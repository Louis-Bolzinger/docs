Podcast automatically edits a synchronized multicamera conversation by detecting who is speaking and selecting an appropriate camera for each moment.

The preparation matters more than any setting. Build a clean synchronized sequence before running the tool.

## Prepare the timeline

<Warning>
  - Create exactly **one audio track with voice clips per speaker**.
  - Each speaker track must contain only that person’s voice, not the full mix.
  - Synchronize every audio and video track before starting.
  - For best results, nest the clips on each audio and video track so each track contains one continuous clip.
  - Avoid placing nested sequences inside those clips.
</Warning>

## Quick Start

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set the speakers and cameras">
    Match the speaker count to the voice tracks and the camera count to the video tracks.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assign each voice track">
    Name every speaker and select the audio track that contains their isolated voice.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Describe each camera">
    For every video track, select the speakers visible in that shot and how frequently the camera should appear.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the cutting method">
    Decide whether unused material should be disabled or cut, and whether the operation should also affect audio.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start the edit">
    Click **Start Editing Podcast** and leave the timeline untouched until processing finishes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Camera assignments

A camera can be assigned to one speaker, several speakers, or all speakers. Use this to describe close-ups, two-shots, and wide shots. The frequency setting controls how often that camera may be selected relative to the others.

<Tip>
  Assign wide shots to every visible speaker and give them a lower frequency than close-ups. They will then act as occasional breathing shots instead of replacing the main coverage.
</Tip>

## Cutting methods

| Method | Result |
|---|---|
| **Disable Clips** | Keeps the original material but disables the portions that are not selected. This is the safest option for later adjustments. |
| **Disable Audio & Clips** | Applies the speaker edit to video and audio. The optional **Link V/A** setting keeps linked material together. |
| **Standard** | Performs a conventional cut-based edit. |

## Advanced settings

| Setting | What it changes |
|---|---|
| **Delay Cuts** | Waits briefly before changing camera so cuts feel less abrupt. |
| **Ignore Small** | Ignores very short speaking turns that would create distracting micro-cuts. |
| **Smoothing** | Stabilizes speaker detection during pauses, room noise, and uncertain transitions. |
| **Min Cut / Max Cut** | Keeps shot lengths within a preferred range. |
| **Frequency Weights** | Controls the relative use of cameras marked High, Medium, Low, or Very Low. |

Saved presets remember the complete setup, which is useful for recurring shows with the same tracks and camera layout.

## Common pitfalls

<Warning>
  - **A full mix on every audio track** prevents reliable speaker detection.
  - **The wrong speaker assigned to a track** produces camera changes for the wrong person.
  - **A camera with no speaker assigned** cannot be selected meaningfully.
  - **Very short shot limits** create a nervous edit; increase Ignore Small or Min Cut.
  - **Editing during processing** can invalidate synchronization and cut positions.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  Start with Disable Clips, moderate smoothing, and conservative camera frequencies. Review the generated edit once, then adjust only the few cuts that do not match your editorial intention.
</Tip>

Subtitles Pro transcribes your sequence and gives you a real editor on top of it: fix a word, move a line break, split or merge captions, run a find & replace, translate into another language, then export an SRT.

It is the **text** half of PremiereGPT's caption tooling. If you want styled, burned-in captions on your timeline, use [Smart Captions](/features/smart-captions) — it shares this exact editor and adds a design step.

Works in **Premiere Pro** and **DaVinci Resolve**.

## Quick Start

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the tool">
    Open **Subtitles Pro** from the Tools Menu, with your sequence open.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the language and the caption limits">
    Pick the spoken **Language** (or leave it on Auto), then set **Max Chars** and **Max Gap** to shape how captions are cut.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Transcribe">
    Click **Transcribe**. The chevron on its left lets you pick a different engine first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Edit">
    Fix anything that's wrong directly in the transcript — words, breaks, punctuation, casing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate SRT">
    Click **Generate SRT**. The file lands in your export folder.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Subtitles Pro handles sequences up to **2 hours**. Longer ones are refused before any work starts.
</Note>

## Settings

### General

| Setting | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Max Chars** | 5–100 | Maximum characters per caption. This is the main lever on caption length. |
| **Max Gap** | 0–2 s | Silences shorter than this don't break a caption — the line keeps running across them. |
| **Language** | Auto + list | The **spoken** language of your audio. |

<Tip>
  Naming the language explicitly beats **Auto** whenever you know it — especially on accented speech, technical vocabulary, or audio that starts with music or silence.
</Tip>

**Max Chars** by format:

- **15–25** — one short line at a time, social / vertical style.
- **30–45** — the classic subtitle range. Comfortable to read, matches broadcast habits.
- **50+** — long lines for wide screens and dense dialogue.

**Max Gap** shapes rhythm: a low value cuts a new caption at every small pause (choppy but precise), a high value keeps sentences together across breaths (smoother, longer captions).

### Correction

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Model** | GPT-5.3 or GPT-4.1 — the model that proofreads the raw transcript. |
| **Spelling** | Turn the correction pass on or off. |

<Note>
  The correction pass fixes spelling, punctuation and obvious transcription slips. Turn it **off** only if you want the raw output untouched — for example when you'll be conforming to an existing script by hand.
</Note>

### SRT Export

| Setting | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Lines** | 1–3 | How many lines each subtitle is wrapped onto. |
| **Solo Punct.** | on / off | Keeps punctuation from being stranded alone on its own line. |

### AI Context

A free-text box (up to 800 characters) read by the transcription itself. This is where you protect the words a generic model will get wrong.

**What to put in it:**

- **Proper nouns:** *"The company is called Kwerty, the CEO is Anaïs Rouvier."*
- **Product and brand names:** *"Our products: FlowKit, FlowKit Pro, DeskSync."*
- **Jargon and acronyms:** *"We say ARR, MRR, ICP, and 'go-to-market' spelled with hyphens."*
- **Preferred spelling:** *"Write 'e-mail', not 'email'."*
- **Language mixing:** *"French speech with English tech terms — keep the English terms in English."*

**What not to put in it:** styling, caption length, translation requests or editing instructions — none of that is read here. Length is Max Chars; style lives in Smart Captions.

<Tip>
  A list of ten proper nouns in this box removes more errors than any engine upgrade. It is the cheapest quality win in the tool.
</Tip>

## Transcription engines

The chevron to the left of **Transcribe** opens three mutually exclusive engines:

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="PremiereGPT" icon="bolt">
    Standard engine — fast and reliable. The default, and the right choice almost every time.
  </Card>
  <Card title="+ Advanced word precision" icon="crosshairs">
    Re-syncs every word to the audio with forced alignment. Sharper timing, slightly slower, extra credits.
  </Card>
  <Card title="V2" icon="microscope">
    The most accurate engine — and much more expensive. Only worth it when the standard engine genuinely isn't good enough.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Tip>
  Pick by symptom: **wrong words** → V2 (or better AI Context first). **Right words, drifting timing** → advanced word precision. Anything else → standard.
</Tip>

## Editing the transcript

The editor is the point of the tool. Everything below is instant and local — no re-transcription.

| Action | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Click a word** | Edit its text inline. |
| **Click between two words** | Insert or remove a caption break there. |
| **Drag a break** | Move it to another position. |
| **Find & Replace** | Fix a recurring name across the whole transcript in one go. |
| **Punctuation toggle** | Strip all punctuation (commas included) or restore it. |
| **Capitals toggle** | Switch between original casing and all-lowercase. |
| **Split at punctuation** | Automatically break captions at punctuation marks. |

<Tip>
  Fix recurring errors with **Find & Replace** before touching anything by hand — a misheard brand name usually appears a dozen times.
</Tip>

## Translation

Pick a target language in the widget at the bottom right and click **Translate**. The translation is created as a **new version**: the arrows next to it let you move between versions, and the original is never lost.

<Note>
  Each version keeps its own timing, so you can generate an SRT per language from the same transcription without re-running it.
</Note>

## Importing an existing SRT

**Import SRT** loads an existing subtitle file as the working transcription. Use it to fix, re-time, translate or re-style subtitles that came from elsewhere, without paying for a transcription.

## Common Pitfalls

<Warning>
  - **Sequence longer than 2 hours** → refused up front. Split it, or work sequence by sequence.
  - **Language left on Auto with a multilingual take** → mixed-up output. Set the dominant language and describe the mixing in AI Context.
  - **Proper nouns wrong after a run** → they belong in AI Context; re-transcribing without it changes nothing.
  - **Music or loud background under the voice** → transcription quality drops. Mute those tracks before running.
  - **Editing after translating** → each version is independent. Fix the source first, then translate.
  - **AI Context over 800 characters** is cut off. Keep it to the words that actually matter.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  **Recommended workflow:** set the language → write ten lines of AI Context → standard engine → transcribe → Find & Replace the recurring errors → adjust Max Chars → Generate SRT.
</Tip>

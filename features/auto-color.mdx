Auto Color grades your timeline like a colorist: it looks at an actual frame from every source clip, measures it, corrects the exposure and white balance, sculpts the contrast, and applies a look on top — while keeping shots from the same scene consistent with each other so your cuts don't jump.

Works in **Premiere Pro** and **DaVinci Resolve**.

## Quick Start

<Steps>
  <Step title="Convert your Log footage first">
    Shooting Log? Apply your **Log → Rec.709 conversion** (LUT or color-space transform) **before** running. Auto Color grades on top of your base image.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the tool">
    Open the **Tools Menu** and select **Auto Color**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Describe the look you want">
    Type your instruction in the **prompt box** — e.g. *"warm cinematic look, match all the clips"*. Optional, but this is where the creative direction lives.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set a time range (optional)">
    Leave **Time Start** and **Time End** empty to grade the whole timeline.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run">
    Click **Start Auto Color**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  **Log / flat footage without a conversion will not grade well.** Auto Color reads the image it is given: if you hand it a washed-out Log frame, it corrects a washed-out Log frame. Put your LUT or color-space transform on first.
</Warning>

## What it actually does

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="1. Shot matching" icon="object-group">
    Every source is fingerprinted, then shots that belong to the same scene, lighting setup or camera are grouped so they share one consistent look. Unrelated scenes are deliberately **not** matched — a daylight exterior and a tungsten interior stay separate.
  </Card>
  <Card title="2. Per-shot grade" icon="palette">
    Each source is then graded individually: correction first (exposure, white balance, contrast), creative look second — following its group's shared direction when it has one.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### What gets adjusted

The grade is a full **Basic Correction** pass, not a two-slider nudge:

| Control | What it's used for |
|---|---|
| **Exposure** | Bring the image to a balanced brightness. |
| **Temperature / Tint** | Neutralise the measured color cast — or push it for the look. |
| **Contrast** | Add shape, especially on flat footage. |
| **Blacks / Whites** | Set the black and white points — this is what makes an image feel filmic instead of milky. |
| **Shadows / Highlights** | Open crushed shadows, recover blown highlights. |
| **Saturation** | Punch or mute, with skin tones protected. |

<Note>
  **One frame per source is analysed**, not one per clip. If the same source appears eight times in your edit, it's analysed once and the grade is applied to all eight instances — consistently, and for the cost of one.
</Note>

### How it lands on your timeline

| Host | Where the grade goes |
|---|---|
| **Premiere Pro** | On **adjustment layers** placed on an empty video track above your clips — fully non-destructive. Your original clips are untouched. |
| **DaVinci Resolve** | Applied per clip on the first node. |

<Tip>
  In Premiere, hide or delete the adjustment-layer track to see the before/after instantly — and to throw the whole grade away in one move.
</Tip>

## Settings

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Prompt box** | Free-text creative direction. Overrides the defaults. |
| **Time Start (s)** | Only grade clips at or after this time. |
| **Time End (s)** | Only grade clips up to this time. |

<Note>
  Unlike Auto Mix, the time range here scopes **both the analysis and the grade** — only the clips in the window are looked at. Grading a section on its own means it won't be matched against shots outside the window, so expect it to sit slightly differently from the rest.
</Note>

## Writing a Good Prompt

Describe the **image you want to see**. Reference a mood, a film, a palette — not slider values.

### What works

- **A look:** *"Warm cinematic look, teal shadows, film-like contrast."* / *"Clean and neutral, corporate."* / *"Cold, desaturated, moody."*
- **A reference:** *"Nordic thriller palette."* / *"Sunny golden-hour vlog feel."* / *"Documentary — natural, no stylisation."*
- **Matching intent:** *"Make everything match, it's all one interview."* / *"Keep each location distinct, don't match across scenes."*
- **A known problem:** *"My footage is too warm under this lighting, neutralise it."* / *"The interior shots are underexposed."*
- **Restraint:** *"Correction only, no creative look."* / *"Subtle — don't push the saturation."*
- **Skin priority:** *"Talking head — skin tones are the priority, everything else is secondary."*

### What doesn't work

- **Slider values.** *"Set exposure to +0.4"* — say the image is dark instead.
- **Per-clip instructions.** *"Make the third shot cooler"* — grade the timeline, then adjust that clip by hand. It's already an editable Lumetri instance.
- **Timecodes in the prompt.** Use the Time Start / Time End fields.
- **Selective / secondary corrections.** No masks, no power windows, no "only change the sky" or "isolate the red jacket". This is a primary grade.
- **Split-toning by name.** *"Teal shadows, orange highlights"* is understood as a direction, but true wheel-based split-toning isn't part of what gets applied — expect the spirit, not the exact technique.
- **Fixing the shot.** Clipped highlights that hold no data, heavy noise, or a badly out-of-focus frame can't be recovered by a grade.
- **A LUT by name.** *"Apply Kodak 2383"* won't load a LUT. Describe the look and it will be approximated with the correction controls.

### Recommended pattern

> *"[The look you want]. [Matching intent]. [Any known problem]. [How far to push it]."*

**Example:**

> *"Warm, filmic look with deep blacks — golden-hour feel. All the interview shots are the same setup, match them. The B-roll outside is a bit cold, neutralise it. Keep it subtle, skin must stay natural."*

<Tip>
  If the first pass overshoots, add one restraint sentence (*"much more subtle, correction-led"*) and re-run, rather than rewriting the whole brief.
</Tip>

## After the run

The grade is standard, editable color correction — nothing is baked in. You can retune any shot, copy a grade from one clip to another, or delete it entirely.

<Tip>
  Review on your cuts, not clip by clip. A grade that looks great in isolation but jumps at a cut is the real failure mode — and it usually means two shots that should have been matched weren't. Say so in the prompt and re-run.
</Tip>

## Common Pitfalls

<Warning>
  - **Log footage with no conversion applied** → the most common cause of a disappointing result. Convert first.
  - **An already-graded timeline** → Auto Color adds its own correction on top of yours. Remove your existing grade first, or the two stack.
  - **Nested sequences** → the clips inside can't be analysed as separate sources. Flatten first.
  - **Expecting scene-to-scene uniformity** → grouping is deliberately conservative. Different lighting setups stay different unless you explicitly ask to match everything.
  - **A very large timeline** costs proportionally more: the price scales with the number of **unique sources**, not the number of clips or the duration.
  - **Frames that can't be exported** (footage offline, media on a blocked or synced folder) are skipped — check your media is online before running.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  **Recommended workflow:** convert Log → one full pass with a short look-and-matching prompt → review at the cuts → re-run with a restraint or matching correction if needed → hand-tune the two or three shots that still stand out.
</Tip>
